The problem

For years, industries in the Baddi-Nalagarh-Barotiwala belt of Himachal Pradesh have been accused of discharging untreated waste that reaches Punjab through the Sirsa and Sutlej rivers. The issue resurfaced sharply in 2026, drawing political attention and renewed public anger.

Case study timeline
  • A Rupnagar MLA filed a formal police complaint in August 2026 alleging industrial waste from Himachal's Baddi-Nalagarh belt was contaminating the Sutlej.
  • In September 2026, the same MLA publicly urged Himachal Pradesh, Haryana, and the Centre to act on untreated effluent entering the river from upstream units.
  • A separate, years-long grassroots campaign known as Kale Pani da Morcha continues to demand action on Sutlej and Budha Nala pollution.
  • Farmers who depend on the river for irrigation report damaged crops and worsening soil quality downstream.

Who this affects

The consequences of the pollution extend well beyond the riverbank itself.

Public health

Communities along the Sutlej and Budha Nala report skin conditions and waterborne illness linked to contaminated water sources.

Agriculture

Farmers irrigating with river water describe stunted crops and degraded soil, threatening a region that depends heavily on farming.

Community trust

Repeated complaints to pollution control boards with little visible enforcement have fuelled frustration and cross-border tension.

What could actually help

  • Mandatory, independently audited effluent treatment for all units in the Baddi-Nalagarh belt before discharge reaches the Sirsa river.
  • A joint Punjab-Himachal Pradesh monitoring body with real enforcement power, not just complaint-forwarding.
  • Public, real-time water quality dashboards for the Sutlej and Budha Nala, so contamination spikes are visible immediately.
  • Health screening camps for communities in the worst-affected downstream villages.
